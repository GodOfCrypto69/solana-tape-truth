The Solana memecoin tape is lying to you. Here's by how much.

Published 2026-08-06. All numbers below were measured on our own full-firehose index of Solana DEX activity (5.5B trade rows, Dec 2025 – Apr 2026, plus live capture since) and are reproducible from public chain state — every claim links to a verification recipe.

Three numbers we measured that nobody publishes:

  • +28.4% — median inflation of apparent buy-side SOL flow vs what actually entered the pool, on momentum-signal windows (P90: +50.5%). 78.9% of such windows are >15% inflated.
  • 97.8% — share of multi-instruction all-buy Pump.fun bundle transactions (99.97% SOL-weighted) that move the pool by only ~ONE instruction's worth: deliberate buy-pressure spoofing.
  • 1.19% — fraction of observed Pump.fun buy intents that land successfully during contested launches (63.5% land as failures, 35.3% never land). The visible tape is ~1% of attempted demand.

Board A: manufactured buy pressure

The problem

Every dashboard, terminal and trading bot displays "buy volume" computed from per-instruction data — because that is what indexers emit. Spoofers exploit exactly this: a single transaction carrying N buy instructions from N different wallets shows up as N buys totalling N× the SOL, while the bonding curve moves by roughly one instruction's worth. The fake flow is engineered to appear precisely where momentum traders and bots look: on our measured data, spoofed transactions are ~6× enriched inside buy-dominance signal windows vs the 3.2% base rate across all Pump.fun buy flow.

The method

Per-transaction reserve-delta reconciliation. For each transaction touching a token's curve: group its trade instructions, sum the claimed buy SOL, and compare against the actual change in the pool's real_sol_reserves — public chain state. Real flow per transaction = the reserve delta; anything claimed above that is manufactured. No wallet clustering, no heuristics, no ML: it is arithmetic on public state. Our implementation was certified against an adversarial parity harness: 0.000% median error vs reserve-delta ground truth on the windows where spoofing concentrates.

Verify it yourself

Take any "sample proof tx" from the scoreboard. On any explorer: (1) count the buy instructions and sum their SOL amounts; (2) read the bonding-curve account's SOL reserves immediately before and after the transaction; (3) subtract. The claimed flow will exceed the reserve move — that difference is the spoof. Anyone with an RPC node or a Dune account can recompute the entire board.

Honest caveats: (a) a high manufactured-% is not proof of wrongdoing by the token team — any third party can bundle-spoof a token; (b) wash trading (offsetting buy+sell inside a window) also lowers reserve-verified flow and is counted as manufactured — that is intentional, since it is equally fake demand; (c) the board covers Pump.fun bonding-curve tokens; post-graduation AMM legs are out of scope for the free board.

Board B: contest & landing telemetry

The problem

Trade feeds only show transactions that succeeded. During a contested launch the overwhelming majority of buy attempts fail or never land — we measured only 1.19% of observed buy intents landing successfully (via a decoded-shred pre-landing capture, 493M intents over 13.2 days, cross-checked against a 503.8M-row failed-transaction archive). What that means for you: in a contested token your market order lands late and fills wide. We measured it live: stop-loss orders set at −30% filled at −39.6% mean — roughly 10 points of pure contest slippage — and 7.2% of sell attempts failed outright (measured over 17k+ live sells).

The method

We index failed Pump.fun transactions — the ones every trade feed drops — and compute per-token, per-30-min: failed-buy count, unique wallets, fee burn, and the attempt-landing rate. The slippage bands on the board are anchored on the live fill measurements above. Failed transactions are public chain data; every row links a sample failed tx you can open on any explorer.

Honest caveat: contest telemetry is an execution-quality signal, not an entry signal. We tested the entry-signal version to destruction (dozens of gated variants across 9 populations) — it does not pick winners. It tells you how badly you will get filled, what tip to pay, and when not to send. That claim we can defend.
